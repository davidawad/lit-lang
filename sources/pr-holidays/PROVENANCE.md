# Legal-holiday statute provenance (pr-holidays)

### `pr-holidays/pr-legal-holidays.txt` — Puerto Rico holiday laws: Ley Núm. 88 de 1969 (1 L.P.R.A. § 84) and Ley Núm. 3 de 1931 (1 L.P.R.A. § 74)

- Source: https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/88-1969.pdf and https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/3-1931.pdf (virtual library of the Oficina de Gerencia y Presupuesto (Office of Management and Budget), Government of Puerto Rico, 'Leyes de Referencia - Días Festivos'). Each PDF carries OGP's own note that it is a compilation by OGP library staff, that the PDF is not an official compilation of the statute, and refers users to the original laws and the L.P.R.A. Ley 88 lists the holidays with their Monday transfers (Martin Luther King, Presidents'/Próceres, Abolition of Slavery March 22, Memorial Day, July 4, Constitution Day July 25, Columbus/Día de la Raza, Labor Day, Veterans Day, Puertorriqueñidad November 19); Ley 3 of 1931 declares January 6. The base list of Puerto Rico's other legal holidays (1 L.P.R.A. § 71, e.g. January 1 and December 25, and other later acts such as Ley 26-2017 for public employees) was NOT found on an official host and is not vendored; the governor may also fix the observed Monday or Friday by an annual proclamation due by January 15 (stated in Ley 88).
- Edition: both PDFs are footed 'Rev. 15 de abril de 2024' (OGP revision date); Ley 88 lists amendments through Ley Núm. 117 de 23 de diciembre de 2022. Any later amendment is not reflected: CURRENCY UNCONFIRMED after April 15, 2024.
