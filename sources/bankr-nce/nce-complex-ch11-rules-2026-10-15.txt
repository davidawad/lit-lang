                       UNITED STATES BANKRUPTCY COURT
                     EASTERN DISTRICT OF NORTH CAROLINA

                     RULES FOR COMPLEX CHAPTER 11 CASES
                              (Effective 10-15-2026)


                                           PART I
                                        APPLICATION

(a)   Pursuant to section 105(d)(2) of Title 11 of the United States Code (the “Bankruptcy
      Code”), unless otherwise ordered, these procedures (the “Complex Case Procedures”)
      apply to the administration of the chapter 11 cases designated as complex cases (herein
      referred to as “Complex Cases”) under the following processes:
      (1)     any case, other than a single asset real estate case, with noncontingent, liquidated
              debt in excess of $25 million in which the debtor elects treatment as a complex case
              by filing a notice of such election with the petition or within 7 days after the order
              for relief; or
      (2)     any case the court designates as a Complex Case.
(b)   Nothing herein prohibits the debtor from (i) requesting a determination that any or all of
      these procedures not apply to a Complex Case; (ii) requesting alternative or additional
      procedures apply; or (iii) seeking such other or further relief as may be just and proper
      under the circumstances.
(c)   The Bankruptcy Code, the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure (the “Bankruptcy
      Rules”), and the Local Rules of the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Eastern District
      of North Carolina (the “Local Bankruptcy Rules”) govern all matters not covered by these
      specific procedures set forth herein.


                                        PART II
                          CASE DESIGNATION ELECTION PROCESS

(a)   ELECTION. The debtor of a chapter 11 case meeting the definition and requirements of a
      Complex Case may make an election to designate the case as a Complex Case subject to
      these procedures by filing a notice of such election contemporaneously with the petition.
(b)   CHALLENGE TO ELECTION. Any party in interest may file a challenge to the election
      by filing an objection to the notice within 14 days of its filing. The court may hold an
      expedited hearing on the matter.
(c)   MOTION TO BE DECLARED A COMPLEX CASE. Within 21 days of the order for
      relief or appointment of a chapter 11 trustee, the debtor-in-possession or trustee may file a
      motion with the court to be treated as a Complex Case. When designation is sought by
      motion, the designation will not be applicable to the case until ordered so by the court.
                                           PART III
                                    FIRST DAY HEARINGS

(a)   Counsel for the debtor in a Complex Case should contact the courtroom staff email for the
      assigned judge as early as possible to obtain a date and time for first day hearings.
(b)   Unless otherwise ordered, first day hearings will be conducted in person. Upon permission
      of the presiding judge, interested parties may participate in first day hearings by video
      conference or teleconference.


                                          PART IV
                                    FIRST DAY MOTIONS

(a)   MOTIONS DECIDED ON LAW AND EVIDENCE. The fact that a motion is listed in this
      Part IV does not mean that the court will grant the relief requested in such a motion, in any
      particular case. Motions will be decided by the court based on applicable law and the
      evidence, including the particular facts and circumstances of the chapter 11 case.
(b)   FIRST DAY ORDERS. “First Day Orders” are orders the debtor seeks to have entered by
      the court, whether on an interim or final basis, on or shortly after the filing of the petition.
      The request for a First Day Order should be made by motion (a “First Day Motion”), and
      a copy of the proposed First Day Order should be filed with and attached as an exhibit to
      the First Day Motion.
(c)   REQUEST FOR RELATED RELIEF NEED NOT BE FILED IN SEPARATE MOTIONS.
      Motions for related relief under First Day Orders referred to above need not be filed as
      separate motions.
(d)   AFFIDAVIT IN SUPPORT. First Day Motions must be supported by an affidavit
      including, at a minimum, the following information if the debtor intends to continue to
      operate its business:
      (1)     the estimated amount of the weekly payroll to employees (exclusive of officers,
              directors, stockholders and partners) for the thirty day period following the filing
              of the chapter 11 petition;
      (2)     the amount paid and proposed to be paid for services for the thirty day period
              following the filing of the chapter 11 petition –
              (A)     if the debtor is a corporation, to officers, stockholders and directors;
              (B)     if the debtor is an individual or a partnership, to the individual or the
                      members of the partnership; and
              (C)     if a financial or business consultant has been retained by the debtor, to the
                      consultant; and
      (3)     a schedule, for the thirty day period following the filing of the chapter 11 petition,
              of estimated cash receipts and disbursements, net cash gain or loss, obligations and
              receivables expected to accrue but remain unpaid, other than professional fees, and
              any other information relevant to an understanding of the foregoing.
(e)   APPROVAL OF INTERIM COMPENSATION PROCEDURE. The order establishing
      procedures for interim compensation for services rendered and reimbursement of expenses
      incurred by retained professionals may include the following provisions:
      (1)     MONTHLY FEE STATEMENTS.
(A)   On or before the 21st day of each calendar month following the month for
      which compensation is sought, or as soon as is practicable thereafter, each
      retained professional seeking interim allowance of its fees and expenses
      must file with the court and serve a monthly fee statement (a “Monthly Fee
      Statement”), by hand delivery, overnight delivery, or email, on the
      following parties (collectively, the “Fee Notice Parties”): (i) the debtor, (ii)
      counsel to the debtor, (iii) the Bankruptcy Administrator (“BA”), (iv)
      counsel for any postpetition lender, and (v) counsel for any official
      committee appointed in the case.
(B)   Any retained professional that fails to file and serve a Monthly Fee
      Statement for a particular month or months may subsequently file and serve
      a consolidated Monthly Fee Statement that includes a request for
      compensation earned or expenses incurred during previous months.
(C)   The deadline for any Fee Notice Party to object to any Monthly Fee
      Statement is the 14th day (or the next business day if such day is not a
      business day) following the date the Monthly Fee Statement is served (the
      “Objection Deadline”); provided, however, nothing herein should be
      construed to prevent the BA from seeking, by consent or order, an extension
      of the Objection Deadline to up to 21 days following service of a Monthly
      Fee Statement.
(D)   To object to a retained professional’s Monthly Fee Statement, the objecting
      Fee Notice Party must file with the court a written objection (“Objection”)
      setting forth, with specificity, the nature of the Objection and the amount of
      fees or expenses at issue on or before the Objection Deadline and serve the
      Objection upon the affected retained professional and each of the Fee Notice
      Parties such that each Fee Notice Party actually receives the Objection on
      or before the Objection Deadline.
(E)   Upon the expiration of the Objection Deadline, the debtor will promptly pay
      the retained professional 70% of the fees and 100% of the expenses
      requested in the applicable Monthly Fee Statement not subject to an
      Objection.
(F)   If a portion of the fees and expenses requested in a Monthly Fee Statement
      is subject to an Objection and the parties are able to resolve the Objection,
      the objecting Fee Notice Party must file with the court and serve upon each
      other Fee Notice Party a statement indicating that the Objection is
      withdrawn. Thereafter, the debtor must promptly pay 70% of the fees and
      100% of the expenses of the Monthly Fee Statement no longer subject to an
      Objection.
(G)   If a portion of the fees and expenses requested in a Monthly Fee Statement
      is subject to an Objection and the parties are unable to reach a consensual
      resolution, the retained professional may either (i) file with the court a
      response to the Objection, together with a request for payment of any
      portion of the amounts subject to the Objection, or (ii) forgo payment of
      such amounts until the next hearing to consider interim or final fee
      applications, at which time the court will adjudicate any unresolved
      Objections.
      (H)  The filing of an Objection to a Monthly Fee Statement will not prejudice
           the objecting Fee Notice Party’s right to object to any Interim Fee
           Application (as defined below) on any ground whether raised in the
           Objection or not. Furthermore, failure by a Fee Notice Party to object to a
           Monthly Fee Statement does not constitute a waiver of any kind nor
           prejudice that Fee Notice Party’s right to object to any Interim Fee
           Application subsequently filed by a retained professional.
      (I)  For purposes of this procedure, retained professionals includes only
           professionals retained by the debtor. Other professionals compensated by
           the estate may move for court authority to seek compensation under this
           procedure.
(2)   INTERIM COMPENSATION APPLICATIONS.
      (A)  Commencing with applications covering the period from the petition date
           through the last day of the month that is two months following the end of
           the month in which the petitions were filed, and at three-month intervals
           thereafter, retained professionals must file with the court an application (an
           “Interim Fee Application”) for interim approval and allowance of
           compensation and reimbursement of expenses sought by such retained
           professional in its Monthly Fee Statements, including any amounts
           requested in Monthly Fee Statements but yet unpaid, filed during the
           preceding interim period (each such period, an “Interim Fee Period”).
      (B)  Retained professionals must file and serve upon the Core Group (as defined
           in Part V(b)(1)), their applicable Interim Fee Applications on or before the
           45th day, or the next business day if such day is not a business day, following
           the end of each Interim Fee Period.
      (C)  The Interim Fee Application must include a brief description identifying the
           following:
           (i)     the Monthly Fee Statements that are the subject of the request;
           (ii)    the amount of fees and expenses requested;
           (iii) the amount of fees and expenses paid to date or subject to an
                   Objection;
           (iv)    the deadline for parties to file Objections to the Interim Fee
                   Application; and
           (v)     any other information requested by the court or required by the
                   Bankruptcy Code, the Bankruptcy Rules, or the Local Bankruptcy
                   Rules.
      (D)  Objections to any Interim Fee Application must be filed with the court and
           served upon the affected retained professional and each of the Fee Notice
           Parties so as to be actually received on or before the 21st day (or the next
           business day if such day is not a business day), following service of the
           applicable Interim Fee Application.
      (E)  The debtor may request that the court schedule a hearing on Interim Fee
           Applications at least once every three months or at such other intervals as
           the court deems appropriate. If no Objections are pending and timely filed,
           the court may approve and allow an Interim Fee Application without a
           hearing. Upon allowance by the court of a retained professional’s Interim
                   Fee Application, the debtor is authorized to promptly pay such retained
                   professional all requested fees (including any holdback from prior Monthly
                   Fee Statements not subject to a pending Objection) and expenses not
                   previously paid.
            (F)    Each retained professional that is an attorney must comply with the BA’s
                   requests for information and additional disclosures as set forth in the
                   Administrative Guide to Practice and Procedure of United States
                   Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of North Carolina, in connection with
                   each fee application.
(f)   ORDINARY COURSE PROFESSIONALS.
      (1)  A motion to approve retention of professionals utilized in the ordinary course of
           business should provide a non-exhaustive list of the debtor’s ordinary course
           professionals.
      (2)  The proposed order establishing procedures for the retention and employment of
           ordinary course professionals may include the following provisions:
           (A)     Each ordinary course professional (the “Ordinary Course Professional”)
                   must provide the following to debtor’s counsel: a declaration (the “OCP
                   Declaration”) that at a minimum provides the information required by
                   Bankruptcy Rule 2014 and certifies that the OCP does not represent or hold
                   any interest adverse to the debtor or their estate with respect to the matters
                   on which such professional is to be employed.
           (B)     Upon receipt of the OCP Declaration, the debtor will file the same with the
                   court and serve the following parties (collectively, the “Reviewing
                   Parties”): (i) the debtor, (ii) counsel to the debtor, (iii) the BA, (iv) counsel
                   for any official committee appointed in the case, and (v) any other interested
                   party as may be directed by the court.
           (C)     If no Objections are filed and properly served within 14 days following the
                   date of service of the OCP Declaration, the retention and employment of
                   such OCP is deemed approved without further order from the court.
           (D)     If an Objection is timely filed and not withdrawn or resolved, the debtor
                   may either resolve the Objection without further notice or court approval or
                   may seek a determination of the dispute by the court by filing a notice for a
                   hearing for the court to consider the Objection.
           (E)     No OCP may be paid any amount for invoiced fees and expenses until such
                   OCP has been retained in accordance with these procedures.
           (F)     The debtor must include in its monthly reports the amounts paid to OCPs.
      (3)  The court may establish a maximum fee that may be charged by an OCP without
           being subject to the procedures set forth in section (e)(2), above. The proposed
           order establishing procedures should include the amount the debtor requests the
           court to set as the maximum fee for purpose of this subsection.
                                                 PART V
                                                SERVICE

(a)   PROCEDURES ESTABLISHED FOR NOTICES. All (a) notices, motions, applications,
      and other requests for relief, (b) briefs, memoranda, affidavits, declarations, replies, and
      other documents filed in support of such papers seeking relief (collectively, “Requests for
      Relief”), and (c) Objections and responses to such Requests for Relief (collectively, the
      “Core Court Filings”) must be filed and served in accordance with the notice procedures
      set forth herein (the “Notice Procedures”). However, nothing in this procedure limits a
      party from seeking court approval for modification to the notice requirements for a specific
      case or matter.
(b)   DEFINITIONS OF SERVICE LISTS.
      (1)     CORE GROUP. The following entities comprise the core group of entities in a
              Complex Case (collectively, the “Core Group”): (a) the BA; (b) the debtor; (c)
              counsel for the debtor; (d) counsel for any committee appointed by the court,1 (e)
              counsel to the debtor’s prepetition lenders; (f) counsel to the debtor’s postpetition
              lenders, if any; (g) all applicable government agencies to the extent required by the
              Bankruptcy Rules and the Local Bankruptcy Rules; and (h) any other party and/or
              counsel designated by the court. Updated Core Group lists will be provided on the
              case website, if any, from time to time.
      (2)     2002 LIST. This group is comprised of all entities that have requested service
              pursuant to Bankruptcy Rule 2002 and the procedures set forth herein.
      (3)     AFFECTED ENTITIES. This group is comprised of all entities with a
              particularized interest in the subject matter of the particular court filing (each, an
              “Affected Entity”).
      (4)     ALL CREDITORS, EQUITY SECURITY HOLDERS AND PARTIES IN
              INTEREST. The mailing matrix as maintained by the court is presumed to be a
              listing of all notice parties under the Bankruptcy Code when so directed. Nothing
              in this procedure, however, prevents a party from challenging the sufficiency of
              such list or moving to reduce or expand same.
(c)   2002 LIST PROCEDURES.
      (1)     ESTABLISHMENT AND MAINTENANCE OF THE LIST. The debtor or the
              claims or noticing agent (the “Claims Agent”) is responsible for maintaining an
              updated list of those that have submitted a proper Bankruptcy Rule 2002 notice
              request (the “2002 List”). Unless the Claims Agent has been authorized by the
              court to maintain the 2002 List in lieu of the debtor, the 2002 List must be filed by
              the debtor within 3 days after the petition date. A revised list must be filed 7 days
              after the initial 2002 List if any revisions are necessary. At least every 15 days
              during the first 60 days of the chapter 11 case, and thereafter at least every 30 days,
              until confirmation of a proposed chapter 11 plan or conversion of the chapter 11
              case to another chapter, the debtor or the Claims Agent must maintain and update
              the 2002 List by: (a) making any additions and deletions as necessary; (b) filing an


      1   Before the appointment of an official committee of unsecured creditors and its counsel, service must be
          made upon either (i) the holders of the debtor’s 20 largest unsecured claims or (ii) in a jointly
          administered chapter 11 case, the consolidated list of the debtor’s [at least] 30 largest creditors.
              updated 2002 List with the court; (c) serving the updated 2002 List on the parties
              listed thereon; (d) filing a proof of service; and (e) simultaneously with the filing
              of the 2002 List, posting an updated version of the 2002 List on the case website,
              if any.
      (2)     REQUEST FOR SERVICE REQUIREMENTS. A request for service of papers
              pursuant to Bankruptcy Rule 2002 (each, a “2002 Notice Request”) filed with the
              court will be deemed proper if and only if it includes the following information with
              respect to the party filing such request: (a) name; (b) street address; (c) name of
              client(s), if applicable; (d) telephone number; (e) facsimile number, if applicable;
              and (f) either (i) electronic mail (or email) address or (ii) a certification in
              compliance with Part V(c)(3) infra.
      (3)     CERTIFICATION OPTING OUT OF EMAIL SERVICE. Any individual or entity
              filing a 2002 Notice Request who does not maintain (and cannot practicably obtain)
              an email address and therefore cannot receive service by email must include in the
              2002 Notice Request a certification to that effect (the “Certification”). The
              Certification must include a statement certifying that the individual or entity does
              not maintain an email address and cannot practicably obtain an email address at
              which the individual or entity could receive service by email.
      (4)     FAILURE TO INCLUDE EMAIL ADDRESS OR CERTIFICATION. If a 2002
              Notice Request fails to include an email address or a Certification, within 5 business
              days after the filing of the Notice Request, such party will not be added to the 2002
              List.
      (5)     It is the responsibility of each entity submitting a 2002 Notice Request to file with
              the court an updated 2002 Notice Request as necessary to reflect changes to any
              information, including email address and contact person, and serve a copy of such
              request upon the debtor.
(d)   SERVICE OF CORE COURT FILINGS. Other than as set forth specifically below in
      Subparagraph (f) or designated by the court, all Core Court Filings must be served on
      members of the Core Group.
(e)   SERVICE OF NOTICE OF MOTIONS. Other than as set forth specifically below in
      Subparagraph (f) or designated by the court, any Notice of Motion must be served on the
      2002 List.
(f)   PROCEEDINGS NOT GOVERNED BY THE NOTICE PROCEDURES. Except as set
      forth herein or otherwise provided by order of the court, the Notice Procedures do not apply
      to notices of the matters or proceedings described in the following Bankruptcy Rules:
      (1)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(a)(1) (meeting of creditors pursuant to section 341 of the
              Bankruptcy Code);
      (2)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(a)(7) (time fixed for filing a proof of claim pursuant to
              Bankruptcy Rule 3003(c));
      (3)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(b)(1) (time fixed for filing objections and any hearing to
              consider approval of a disclosure statement);
      (4)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(b)(2) (time fixed for filing objections and any hearing to
              consider confirmation of a chapter 11 plan);
      (5)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(d) (certain matters for which notice is to be provided to
              equity security holders);
      (6)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(f)(1) (entry of an order for relief);
      (7)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(f)(2) (dismissal or conversion of a case to another
              chapter of the Bankruptcy Code);
      (8)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(f)(3) (time allowed for filing claims pursuant to Bankruptcy
              Rule 3002);
      (9)     Bankruptcy Rule 2002(f)(6) (waiver, denial, or revocation of a discharge as
              provided in Bankruptcy Rule 4006);
      (10) Bankruptcy Rule 2002(f)(7) (entry of an order confirming a chapter 11 plan); and
      (11) Bankruptcy Rule 2002(f)(8) (summary of the trustee’s final report and account
              should a case be converted to chapter 7 of the Bankruptcy Code).
(g)   CERTIFICATES OF SERVICE. Notwithstanding Local Bankruptcy Rule 5005-4(9),
      “Service of Documents by Electronic or Conventional Means; Calculation of Response
      Time,” certificates of service of all Core Filings, including a Service List, must be filed by
      the party seeking relief with the court within 7 days of the completion of noticing any
      particular matter; provided, however, that parties are not required to include the Service
      List when serving the certificate of service to such recipients.
(h)   SERVICE BY ELECTRONIC MAIL. All filings, other than a summons and complaint in
      an adversary proceeding or documents filed under seal, will be electronically served by the
      court’s electronic filing system, which is deemed to constitute proper service for all parties
      who are sent such email service. Subject to the limited exclusions set forth herein, each
      party that has filed a notice of appearance and a request for service of papers is deemed to
      have consented to electronic service of papers and service on this party is deemed proper
      upon a filing of a certificate of service stating such method was used.


                                         PART VI
                                      HEARING DATES

(a)   Unless otherwise directed by the court, omnibus hearing dates and non-omnibus hearing
      dates may be obtained by contacting the assigned judge’s courtroom staff email.
(b)   OMNIBUS HEARINGS. Periodic omnibus hearings will occur as may be scheduled by
      the court (the “Omnibus Hearings”). Throughout the Complex Case, the debtor may
      periodically request that Omnibus Hearings be scheduled as necessary. The debtor must
      send notices of the Omnibus Hearings to the Core Group and 2002 List when the Omnibus
      Hearings are scheduled and file notices of additional Omnibus Hearing dates on a periodic
      basis as set by the court.
      (1)     PROCEDURES REGARDING OMNIBUS HEARINGS. Except as specifically
              set forth herein, all notice periods for Requests for Relief will be computed in
              accordance with the Bankruptcy Rules and Local Bankruptcy Rules and nothing in
              these procedures changes those requirements.
      (2)     PROPOSED AGENDA FOR OMNIBUS HEARINGS. The debtor must prepare
              Omnibus Hearing agendas in accordance with the following:
              (A)     Debtor’s counsel must file a proposed agenda with regard to matters
                      scheduled to be heard at an Omnibus Hearing (the “Proposed Hearing
                      Agenda”) no later than the date that is 2 calendar days prior to each such
                      Omnibus Hearing.
             (B)     The Proposed Hearing Agenda will include, to the extent known by the
                     debtor’s counsel: (i) the docket number and title of each matter to be
                     scheduled for hearing on such Omnibus Hearing, including the initial filing
                     and any responses, replies, or documents related thereto; (ii) whether the
                     matters are contested or uncontested; (iii) whether the matters have settled
                     or are proposed to be continued; (iv) other comments that will assist the
                     court, including the name of counsel presenting the matter; and (v) a
                     suggestion for the order in which the matters should be addressed.


                                    PART VII
                   PROCEDURES FOR REMOTE HEARING PARTICIPATION

(a)   The court may allow counsel to participate in any hearing by telephone, teleconference or
      video conference.
(b)   Unless otherwise provided in the notice of hearing, parties may participate telephonically
      in in-person hearings without the need to file a motion. Instructions for telephonic
      appearances will be stated in the notice of hearing and motion. A party may appear
      telephonically for argument at an evidentiary hearing; however, parties appearing
      telephonically are not permitted to examine witnesses or submit evidence unless otherwise
      permitted by the judge.
(c)   In the court’s discretion, hearings may be conducted by video conference. While no motion
      is required to authorize participation via video conference, participants must pre-register
      by contacting the assigned judge’s courtroom staff email.
(d)   Under no circumstances may any participant or listener record or broadcast the
      proceedings.


                                PART VIII
         APPLICATION OF LOCAL BANKRUPTCY RULES 3002-1 AND 4002-1

(a)   Local Bankruptcy Rule 3002-1 Chapter 11 Debtor’s Notice of Disputed, Contingent or
      Unliquidated Claims is inapplicable in Complex Cases without the need for the Debtor to
      seek waiver of same.
(b)   Local Bankruptcy Rule 4002-1(c)(3)(B)(i) Bank Accounts is hereby modified as follows
      in Complex Cases:
      (i)    The debtor-in-possession, consistent with 11 U.S.C. § 345, is authorized to open
             and maintain bank accounts for the deposit, investment, and disbursement of
             monies of the estate; provided, however, that the debtor-in-possession must
             segregate all monies withheld from employees or collected for taxes in a separate
             bank account(s) and must pay these funds to the proper authority when due.
(c)   Local Bankruptcy Rule 4002-1(c)(1) is modified as follows in Complex Cases:
      (1)    MONTHLY REPORT.
             (A)     The debtor must file with the clerk (i) monthly accountings or (ii) monthly
                     financial statements (including a profit and loss statement and a balance
                    sheet) in the format regularly maintained by the debtor in its ordinary course
                    of business, the first report being due within 30 days following the end of
                    the month in which the petition was filed or date of conversion from another
                    chapter, and subsequent reports on or before the thirtieth day of each month
                    thereafter. The BA may request or require additional information if it deems
                    the monthly financial statements (item (ii) above) insufficient.
             (B)    The debtor must transmit a copy of all monthly reports (as provided in (A)
                    above) to the BA, attorney for and chair of the unsecured creditors
                    committee, and such other interested parties as may be directed by the court.
                    The content and format of the monthly report is governed by subsection (D).
             (C)    Monthly bank statements must be transmitted to the BA at the time the
                    monthly report is filed with the court. However, the monthly bank
                    statements should not be filed with the court.
             (D)    The reporting requirements for the monthly reports referenced in subsection
                    (A) will be established by the BA.
                    (i)     The BA may, in the exercise of discretion, agree to a modification
                            of the reporting format without further order of the court. Any
                            agreed modification must be memorialized in writing between the
                            debtor and the BA.
                    (ii)    In the event the debtor requests a modification to which the BA does
                            not agree, either party may file a motion with the court seeking a
                            modification of the reporting format. Such motion must set forth
                            with specificity the proposed alternative format.
                    (iii) Any modification of the reporting format, whether agreed to by the
                            BA or ordered by the court, must satisfy the following conditions:
                            (a)     The modified format must contain all certifications required
                                    by the BA; and
                            (b)     The modified format must include all of the information that
                                    is required by the BA's prescribed reporting format.
(d)   Local Bankruptcy Rule 4002-1(d)(1) Payments to Principals is inapplicable in Complex
      Cases without the need for the debtor to seek waiver of same.


                                          PART IX
                                    MONTHLY ACCOUNTING

(a)   REPORTING AND FORMAT. For each day following the petition date until the plan is
      confirmed or the case is dismissed or converted, the debtor-in-possession or trustee must
      file a monthly report setting forth the activities of the estate. The report must be a calendar
      date report regardless of the filing date or ending date of the case. The report will be filed
      with the court and served on the BA, counsel for any committee appointed by the court and
      any party designated by the court for receipt of same.
(b)   DUE DATE. The report is due on the last day of the month following the reporting period
      of the report.
