Rule 31.23. Warrant of Execution

Currentness
(a) Issuance of Warrant. After affirming a death sentence, the Supreme Court must issue a warrant of execution if the State files a notice stating that:
(1) the defendant has not filed a first Rule 32 petition for post-conviction relief and the time for filing a petition has expired;
(2) the defendant has not filed a petition for review seeking review of a superior court denial of the defendant's first Rule 32 petition for post-conviction relief and the time for filing a petition for review has expired; or
(3) the defendant has not initiated habeas corpus proceedings in federal district court within 15 days after the Supreme Court's denial of a petition for review seeking review of the denial of the defendant's first Rule 32 petition for post-conviction relief.
(b) Post-Habeas Warrant. On the State's motion, the Supreme Court must issue a warrant of execution when federal habeas corpus proceedings and habeas appellate review conclude.
(c) Notice of Motion for Warrant of Execution.
(1) Notice. If the State requires a specific timeline for the Court's consideration of a motion for warrant of execution, before filing the motion, the State must file a notice advising the Court and the defendant of the State's intention to file the motion.
(2) Content. The notice must include, as an attachment, a copy of the State's planned motion for warrant of execution. The notice must also state:
(A) a specific date -- not less than 21 calendar days after the notice's filing date -- on which the State seeks to file the motion for warrant;
(B) provide the names and contact information, including email addresses if available, of counsel for each party and of any self-represented party; and
(C) state any dates presently known to the State, between 90 and 150 days after the notice's filing date, on which the Arizona Department of Corrections, Rehabilitation, and Reentry could not feasibly carry out the Court's warrant.
The dates known to the State must account for, although the Court is not limited to these days, a date of execution set on a Wednesday, and where a Wednesday is not feasible on a Thursday, or alternatively, on a Tuesday.
(3) Service. The State must serve the notice as provided in Rule 31.6(c)(1) and must indicate in the notice that it has complied with applicable victims' rights.
(4) Scheduling Conference. Simultaneously with filing the notice under (c)(2), the State must file a written request that the Court set an initial telephonic scheduling conference to determine a schedule for briefing on the State's motion for warrant of execution. The parties must be prepared to address the following topics at the initial scheduling conference:
(A) any pending deadlines that might affect the schedule for briefing of the motion for warrant of execution or the Court's consideration of the motion;
(B) any request for a court order for appointment of counsel for the defendant;
(C) The State's proposed date to file its motion for warrant and deadlines for the defendant's response, if any, and for the State's and crime victim's replies, if any, to the response;
(D) The word limits for the State's motion for warrant and the response and replies;
(E) Any dates presently known to the State, between 90 and 150 days after the filing date of the State's notice under (c)(3), on which the Arizona Department of Corrections, Rehabilitation, and Reentry could not feasibly carry out the Court's warrant.
(5) Scheduling Order. Within 5 days after the initial scheduling conference under (c)(4), the Court will issue an order that:
(A) sets the date on which the State is ordered to file its motion for warrant, the date by which the defendant may file a response, if any, to the motion, and the date by which the State and the crime victim may file a reply, if any, to the response;
(B) if practicable, provides the date the Court anticipates conferencing the motion for warrant, and the anticipated date a warrant of execution will issue if the motion is granted; and
(C) enters any other order the Court deems appropriate, including specific due dates for any third-party responsive pleading or amicus curiae brief.
(6) Other Court Orders. For good cause, the Court may stay, continue, or dismiss a notice filed under (c)(1), or enter other appropriate orders.
(d) Date and Time of Execution. The warrant of execution must specify an execution date that is 35 days after the warrant's issuance. If the Supreme Court finds that it is impracticable to carry out an execution on that date, it may extend the execution date but may not extend it more than 60 days after the warrant's issuance. Additionally, the warrant must:
(1) state the date for starting the execution time period;
(2) state that the warrant is valid for 24 hours beginning at an hour to be designated by the director of the Arizona Department of Corrections;
(3) order the director to provide written notice of the designated hour of execution to the Supreme Court and each party at least 20 calendar days before the execution date; and
(4) authorize the director to carry out the execution at any time during the warrant's duration.
(e) Return on Warrant. The director of the Arizona Department of Corrections must make a return on the warrant to the Supreme Court showing the manner and time of execution.

Credits
